Appendix BPolynomial input–output models

This appendix collects the facts about input–output (polynomial) models of discrete-time LTI systems used in Chapter 6: the shift operator and its relation with the complex variable \(z\), the model structures FIR, ARX, ARMAX, OE and Box–Jenkins written in the time domain and in the operator and \(\Z\) domains, and their relation with transfer functions and with state-space models.

B.1 The shift operator

The forward shift operator \(q\) and the backward shift (delay) operator \(q^{-1}\) act on a signal \(f(k)\) as

\[ q\,f(k)=f(k+1),\qquad q^{-1}f(k)=f(k-1),\qquad q^{-\tau}f(k)=f(k-\tau). \]

They are linear and time invariant, and compositions of shifts behave like powers: \(q^{-i}q^{-j}=q^{-(i+j)}\). A polynomial operator such as \(A(q)=1-a_1q^{-1}-\cdots-a_nq^{-n}\) acts as \(A(q)y(k)=y(k)-a_1y(k-1)-\cdots-a_ny(k-n)\); products of polynomial operators commute and follow the rules of polynomial algebra. A rational operator \(G(q)=B(q)/A(q)\) is defined through the difference equation it represents:

\[ y(k)=\frac{B(q)}{A(q)}u(k)\quad\overset{\text{def}}{\iff}\quad A(q)y(k)=B(q)u(k), \]

with zero initial conditions (or, for asymptotically stable \(A\), with the effect of the initial conditions vanishing), so that \(y(k)=\sum_{j\ge0}g(j)u(k-j)\), where \(G(q)=\sum_jg(j)q^{-j}\) is the expansion of \(B/A\) in powers of \(q^{-1}\) (the impulse response).

\(q\) versus \(z\). \(q^{-1}\) is an operator acting on time sequences; \(z\) is a complex variable. If \(y(k)=G(q)u(k)\) with zero initial conditions, then, by the delay property of the \(\Z\)-transform (\(\Z\{f(k-\tau)\}=z^{-\tau}F(z)\)),

\[ Y(z)=G(z)U(z), \]

where \(G(z)\) is obtained from \(G(q)\) by replacing \(q^{-1}\) with \(z^{-1}\). Formulas written in \(q\) and in \(z\) are therefore formally identical, but \(y(k)=G(z)u(k)\) mixes the two domains and should be avoided (Chapter 6).

Remark B.1 (Alternative conventions). Some textbooks (especially in the Italian tradition of identification and time-series analysis) use the same letter for the delay, writing \(q\,f(k)=f(k-1)\) (or \(z^{-1}\) as an operator, or \(L\) and \(B\) for the lag/backshift operator). In these notes, as in the lecture notes, in Ljung [5], Söderström–Stoica [6] and MATLAB, the delay is \(q^{-1}\), so that \(q^{-1}\leftrightarrow z^{-1}\). When reading other sources, always check which convention is adopted: with the alternative one, a polynomial “\(A(q)=1-a_1q\)” is our \(A(q)=1-a_1q^{-1}\).

B.2 The model structures

All the structures are special cases of the general family (Ljung)

\[A(q)\,y(k)=\frac{B(q)}{F(q)}\,u(k-n_k)+\frac{C(q)}{D(q)}\,e(k), \tag{B.1}\]

where \(e(k)\) is white noise with zero mean and variance \(\lambda_e\), \(n_k\ge0\) is the input delay, and (with the sign convention of the lecture notes for \(A\))

\[ \begin{aligned} A(q)&=1-a_1q^{-1}-\cdots-a_{n_a}q^{-n_a}, & B(q)&=b_0+b_1q^{-1}+\cdots+b_{n_b-1}q^{-n_b+1},\\ C(q)&=1+c_1q^{-1}+\cdots+c_{n_c}q^{-n_c}, & D(q)&=1+d_1q^{-1}+\cdots+d_{n_d}q^{-n_d},\\ F(q)&=1+f_1q^{-1}+\cdots+f_{n_f}q^{-n_f}. \end{aligned} \]

In the notes the delay is embedded in \(B\) (e.g. \(B(q)=b_1q^{-1}+b_2q^{-2}\) when \(n_k=1\)). The block diagrams are in Figure 6.1 and the summary in Table 6.1. For each structure we give the operator form, the difference equation and the \(\Z\)-domain description \(Y(z)=W(z)U(z)+H(z)E(z)\) (zero initial conditions).

FIR (finite impulse response). \(y(k)=B(q)u(k)+e(k)\):

\[ y(k)=b_0u(k)+b_1u(k-1)+\cdots+b_{m}u(k-m)+e(k), \]
\[ W(z)=B(z)=\frac{b_0z^m+b_1z^{m-1}+\cdots+b_m}{z^m},\quad H(z)=1 . \]

All the poles are in \(z=0\); the parameters are the first samples of the impulse response (Section 7.4).

ARX. \(A(q)y(k)=B(q)u(k)+e(k)\):

\[ y(k)=\sum_{i=1}^{n_a}a_iy(k-i)+\sum_{j=0}^{m}b_ju(k-j)+e(k),\qquad W(z)=\frac{B(z)}{A(z)},\quad H(z)=\frac1{A(z)} . \]

Plant and noise share the denominator: the noise enters “at the input of the dynamics” (the equation error \(e(k)\) is white).

ARMAX. \(A(q)y(k)=B(q)u(k)+C(q)e(k)\):

\[ y(k)=\sum_{i=1}^{n_a}a_iy(k-i)+\sum_{j=0}^{m}b_ju(k-j)+e(k)+\sum_{i=1}^{n_c}c_ie(k-i),\qquad W(z)=\frac{B(z)}{A(z)},\quad H(z)=\frac{C(z)}{A(z)} . \]

The equation error is a moving average of the white noise.

Output error (OE). \(y(k)=\frac{B(q)}{F(q)}u(k)+e(k)\): introducing the noise-free output \(w(k)=\frac{B(q)}{F(q)}u(k)\),

\[ w(k)=-\sum_{i=1}^{n_f}f_iw(k-i)+\sum_{j=0}^{m}b_ju(k-j),\qquad y(k)=w(k)+e(k),\qquad W(z)=\frac{B(z)}{F(z)},\quad H(z)=1 . \]

The noise is white measurement noise on the output; this is the input–output counterpart of the state-space model (8.1) used by MOESP.

Box–Jenkins (BJ). \(y(k)=\frac{B(q)}{F(q)}u(k)+\frac{C(q)}{D(q)}e(k)\):

\[ W(z)=\frac{B(z)}{F(z)},\qquad H(z)=\frac{C(z)}{D(z)}, \]

with independently parametrized plant and noise dynamics. In the notes the plant denominator of BJ is called \(A(q)\); with \(F=D=A\) one recovers ARMAX, with \(C=D=1\) one recovers OE, and with \(C=1\), \(F=D=A\) one recovers ARX.

Positive powers of \(z\). To find poles and zeros, multiply numerator and denominator by the appropriate power of \(z\). For instance, for the ARX model of Example 6.2,

\[ W(z)=\frac{z^{-1}+0.5z^{-2}}{1-1.5z^{-1}+0.7z^{-2}}=\frac{z+0.5}{z^2-1.5z+0.7},\qquad H(z)=\frac{z^2}{z^2-1.5z+0.7}: \]

poles \(0.75\pm0.37j\) (shared by \(W\) and \(H\)), a zero of \(W\) in \(-0.5\), and a double zero of \(H\) in \(0\). The relative degree of \(W\) (\(2-1=1\)) equals the input delay \(n_k=1\).

B.3 Relation with transfer functions

For a SISO system with \(x(0)=0\), \(Y(z)=W(z)U(z)\) and (Appendix A)

\[ W(z)=\frac{\beta_mz^m+\cdots+\beta_0}{z^n+\alpha_{n-1}z^{n-1}+\cdots+\alpha_0},\qquad m\le n . \]

Dividing by \(z^n\),

\[ W(z)=\frac{z^{-(n-m)}\bigl(\beta_m+\beta_{m-1}z^{-1}+\cdots+\beta_0z^{-m}\bigr)}{1+\alpha_{n-1}z^{-1}+\cdots+\alpha_0z^{-n}}=\frac{B(z)}{A(z)}, \]

so every proper rational transfer function is the \(W\) of an ARX (or ARMAX, OE) model with \(n_a=n\), \(a_i=-\alpha_{n-i}\) (convention of the notes; \(a_i=+\alpha_{n-i}\) with the MATLAB sign), \(n_k=n-m\) and \(b_j=\beta_{m-j}\). Conversely \(A(q)y(k)=B(q)u(k)\) describes the forced response of every system with transfer function \(B(z)/A(z)\). Note that:

  • \(W\) is strictly proper (\(D=0\)) iff \(b_0=0\), i.e. \(n_k\ge1\);

  • common roots of \(A(z)\) and \(B(z)\) cancel: a polynomial model with non-coprime \(A,B\) has a lower-order transfer function, and its parameters are not identifiable (a whole family of \((A,B)\) gives the same \(W\)): model orders should not be over-estimated;

  • for MIMO systems the same construction works with matrix polynomials, e.g. \(A(q)=I-A_1q^{-1}-\cdots-A_{n_a}q^{-n_a}\) with \(A_i\in\R^{q\times q}\) and \(B(q)=B_0+B_1q^{-1}+\cdots\) with \(B_j\in\R^{q\times p}\) (MIMO ARX models, still estimated by least squares), but their parametrization is less straightforward than that of state-space models.

B.4 Relation with state-space models

From ARX to state space. Consider the (SISO) ARX model \(y(k)=\sum_{i=1}^na_iy(k-i)+\sum_{j=0}^nb_ju(k-j)+e(k)\) (pad with zero coefficients if \(n_a\neq n_b-1\)). Define the state components recursively by

\[ \begin{aligned} x_1(k)&=y(k)-b_0u(k)-e(k),\\ x_i(k+1)&=a_iy(k)+x_{i+1}(k)+b_iu(k),\quad i=1,\dots,n-1,\qquad x_n(k+1)=a_ny(k)+b_nu(k). \end{aligned} \]

Unrolling the recursion reproduces the difference equation. Substituting \(y(k)=x_1(k)+b_0u(k)+e(k)\) one obtains the observer canonical form with noise:

\[\left\{\begin{aligned} x(k+1)&=\underbrace{\bmat{a_1&1&0&\cdots&0\\ a_2&0&1&\cdots&0\\ \vdots&&&\ddots&\vdots\\ a_{n-1}&0&\cdots&0&1\\ a_n&0&\cdots&0&0}}_{A_o}x(k) +\underbrace{\bmat{b_1+a_1b_0\\ b_2+a_2b_0\\ \vdots\\ b_n+a_nb_0}}_{B_o}u(k) +\underbrace{\bmat{a_1\\ a_2\\ \vdots\\ a_n}}_{K}e(k),\\ y(k)&=\underbrace{\bmat{1&0&\cdots&0}}_{C_o}x(k)+b_0u(k)+e(k). \end{aligned}\right. \tag{B.2}\]

Without the noise terms this is a realization of \(W(z)=B(z)/A(z)\) (always observable, \(\det(zI-A_o)=z^n-a_1z^{n-1}-\cdots-a_n\), reachable iff \(A\) and \(B\) are coprime).

The innovation form. Model (B.2) is a special case of the innovation form

\[x(k+1)=Ax(k)+Bu(k)+Ke(k),\qquad y(k)=Cx(k)+Du(k)+e(k), \tag{B.3}\]

the most general stationary LTI model with white innovation \(e\) (\(K\) is the steady-state Kalman gain). Eliminating \(e(k)=y(k)-Cx(k)-Du(k)\) gives the predictor form

\[ x(k+1)=(A-KC)x(k)+(B-KD)u(k)+Ky(k),\qquad \hat y(k|k-1)=Cx(k)+Du(k), \]

and, by the matrix determinant lemma, the SISO noise transfer function is

\[ H(z)=1+C(zI-A)^{-1}K=\frac{\det(zI-A+KC)}{\det(zI-A)} . \]

Therefore, in the SISO case, every innovation model is an ARMAX model with \(A(z)\leftrightarrow\det(zI-A)\) and \(C(z)\leftrightarrow\det(zI-A+KC)\) (both divided by \(z^n\)), and

  • ARX corresponds to \(A-KC\) nilpotent (all eigenvalues in \(0\), i.e. \(C(q)=1\)): in (B.2), \(A_o-KC_o\) is the shift matrix with ones on the superdiagonal. The predictor \(\hat y(k|k-1)=[1-A(q)]y(k)+B(q)u(k)\) is then a finite (deadbeat) filter of the data, which is why it is linear in the parameters;

  • ARMAX corresponds to a general \(K\): in observer form, \(K=(a_1+c_1,\dots,a_n+c_n)\T\);

  • OE corresponds to \(K=0\) in the state equation (the noise only enters the output equation), i.e. the model \(x(k+1)=Ax+Bu\), \(y=Cx+Du+e\) of Chapter 8;

  • BJ requires an augmented state of dimension \(n_f+n_d\) (plant and noise dynamics are distinct), with a block-diagonal \(A\).

From state space to polynomial models. Given \(\{A,B,C,D\}\) (SISO), \(W(z)=C(zI-A)^{-1}B+D=\frac{C\operatorname{adj}(zI-A)B+D\det(zI-A)}{\det(zI-A)}\): the denominator gives \(A(z)\) (after division by \(z^n\) and, if the realization is not minimal, after cancelling common factors, Section A.6), the numerator gives \(B(z)\). In MATLAB: [num,den] = ss2tf(A,B,C,D) and tf2ss for the converse (Control System Toolbox also idpoly/idss conversions in the System Identification Toolbox).

Example B.1: ARX model in state space

For \(y(k)=1.5y(k-1)-0.7y(k-2)+u(k-1)+0.5u(k-2)+e(k)\) (\(b_0=0\)), (B.2) gives

\[ A_o=\bmat{1.5&1\\-0.7&0},\quad B_o=\bmat{1\\0.5},\quad C_o=\bmat{1&0},\quad D=0,\quad K=\bmat{1.5\\-0.7}, \]

and \(A_o-KC_o=\bmat{0&1\\0&0}\) is nilpotent. One checks that \(C_o(zI-A_o)^{-1}B_o=\frac{z+0.5}{z^2-1.5z+0.7}\) and that the simulation of (B.2) coincides with the ARX difference equation for any sequences \(u\) and \(e\) (verified numerically to \(10^{-15}\)).

B.5 Exercises

Exercise B.1. Write the ARMAX model \(y(k)=0.7y(k-1)+u(k-1)+e(k)+0.4e(k-1)\) in innovation form, and verify that the eigenvalue of \(A-KC\) is the root of \(C(z)\).

Exercise B.2. Show that an OE model \(y=\frac{B}{F}u+e\) can be written as an ARMAX model with \(A=C=F\). Why is it nevertheless not convenient to estimate it as an ARMAX model?

Exercise B.3. Using the controllable canonical form, find a state-space realization of the ARX model of Example 6.2 and the change of coordinates relating it to (B.2).

Exercise B.4. A MIMO ARX model with \(q=2\), \(p=1\), \(n_a=1\) has \(A(q)=I_2-A_1q^{-1}\) and \(B(q)=b_1q^{-1}\). Write the least-squares problem for \((A_1,b_1)\) and a state-space realization of order \(2\).
